# Experience quality bar

Use this reference after the brief and before presenting directions.

## Design from a reason, not a recipe

For every visible decision, record a short answer to one of these questions:

- What visitor task does it clarify?
- What supplied brand trait does it express?
- What real proof or product behavior does it make easier to understand?
- What user-selected design choice does it honor?

If it answers none, remove it. Do not use novelty, badges, effects, or motion
as a substitute for a reason.

## Build an authored system

Define a small, page-specific system before implementation:

- semantic palette roles: canvas, ink, muted text, surface, border, action,
  and only necessary accents;
- type roles: display, body, label, numerical/data, and editorial emphasis;
- spatial rhythm: base unit, section cadence, reading measure, and grid logic;
- surface rules: when content is flat, divided, elevated, textured, or image-led;
- image rules: subject, crop, tone, density, and responsive behavior;
- motion rules: trigger, purpose, duration range, easing character, and
  reduced-motion alternative.

Do not select a style name and then decorate generic components. Let the system
change hierarchy, composition, content rhythm, and interaction behavior.

## Intuitive conversion sequence

The first viewport must make the offer, audience relevance, next action, and
available proof understandable without relying on animation. Then arrange only
the sections needed to resolve the visitor’s likely questions:

1. What is this and is it for me?
2. Why should I trust it?
3. How does it produce the promised outcome?
4. What does it cost, require, or change?
5. What is the next low-friction action?

Use specific navigation labels and CTA language that describe the destination or
outcome. Remove a section when it repeats the previous answer.

## Responsive and motion baseline

Design the narrow experience as a first-class composition. Test hierarchy,
touch targets, line length, image crops, navigation, long content, 320px
reflow, and 200% zoom. Images need appropriate sources and reserved space.

Motion may orient, confirm, reveal a relationship, or demonstrate a real
product behavior. It must never be the only way to understand content. Respect
reduced motion, avoid automatic looping distractions, and prefer a small
number of deliberate transitions over an animated page.

## Originality review

Before selection, ask:

- Could the hero, navigation, and section order belong to any unrelated SaaS?
- Would the design still have an identity without blur, gradient, or motion?
- Do two directions differ in structure as well as colour and font?
- Is every piece of proof genuine and positioned next to its claim?
- Does the page become clearer, not merely louder, as it scrolls?
